Know your rights

What the job owes you.

Plain-language starting points, plus the agencies that enforce them. None of this is legal advice — it's what to look up and who to call.

  • Harassment and discriminationYou can file with the EEOC, and in most states with a state agency too. Deadlines are short — often 180 or 300 days.
  • PPE that fitsSince January 13, 2025, OSHA's construction standard (29 CFR 1926.95) explicitly requires employer-provided PPE to properly fit each worker. Gear sized for a six-foot man is a safety violation, not a personal problem. CPWR keeps a list of suppliers who actually make it.
  • Pregnancy and lactationFederal law covers reasonable accommodations and break time. Ask your hall what your agreement adds on top.
  • Bathrooms and facilitiesSanitary facilities are a jobsite requirement, not a courtesy. OSHA takes complaints about this.
  • RetaliationReporting a hazard or filing a complaint is protected activity. Retaliation for it is separately illegal.
  • Your union agreementYour CBA often gives you more than the law does. Your steward and your local's women's committee are the fastest route to an answer.
